# Rubric: sla-four-nines
Pass = every criterion marked [critical] passes and at least 5 of 6 pass overall.
1. [critical] Rejects 99.99% on actual history (99.95%, so Provider would miss it most months) and identifies the target together with the sole-remedy deletion as what drives the decision: a target missed most months becomes a recurring breach once credits are not the sole remedy. (Revised after the 2026-11-17 run; it previously required the target alone to be named as the driver.)
2. [critical] Gives downtime per 30-day month: about 43 minutes at 99.9%, about 4.3 minutes at 99.99%, about 3.6 hours at 99.5%.
3. [critical] Keeps credits as the sole remedy for unavailability (or counters "in addition to"), and explains a chronic-failure termination right is the escape valve.
4. Treats the 99.5% single-month trigger with judgment: either counters to the chronic-failure fallback or notes that, with a worst month of 99.86%, it is low-risk but needs business-owner approval.
5. Notes SLA terms are commercial and owned by sales, while still giving a substantive verdict.
6. Answer is usable by the account executive.
