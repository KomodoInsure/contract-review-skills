# SaaS and cloud services

Start from the actual service and deployment. Identify hosted access versus a software license, users/affiliates, usage limits, implementation, integrations, support, term, billing and renewal. Read the order, standard terms, SLA, DPA, security commitments and amendments as one packet with explicit precedence. A label such as “standard SaaS MSA” is not evidence of any particular allocation.

## Non-obvious checks
- An implementation milestone, subscription start and billing start may differ. Determine which dependency each date assumes and what happens if the customer delays cooperation.
- Service credits may be the exclusive remedy for a defined availability failure, while a separate termination right applies to persistent failure. Preserve their scopes; do not assume either eliminates all contractual claims.
- Price notice must leave a usable exit window. Compute deadlines using the actual notice rule and calendar/business-day convention; request the missing renewal date if necessary.
- Suspension can interrupt exports and business continuity. Review triggers, notice/cure where applicable, urgency exceptions, reinstatement and continuing fees together.
- A “fees paid” cap can be small early in the term; distinguish paid, payable, all orders and the affected service. A lookback anchored to a claim is different from one anchored to an event.
- Separate use needed to deliver the service from product improvement, model training, marketing, benchmarking and onward disclosure. Resolve customer content, usage data and feedback definitions before judging permissions.
- A DPA can control personal-data duties without overriding every commercial liability provision. Read the actual priority scope and any liability language.
- On exit, check the export window, usable format, assistance, cost, continued access, deletion timing and backup/legal-retention exceptions. Do not promise technical behavior without owner confirmation.

## Review output
Translate technical and commercial facts into consequences: a customer cannot exercise non-renewal after learning the new price; a provider cannot meet an unsupported deletion commitment; an export right may be unusable after immediate suspension. Prefer those explanations to generic “vendor-friendly/customer-friendly” labels.
