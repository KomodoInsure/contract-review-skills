# Indemnification and claim handling

Separate **coverage** from **procedure**. Read both against liability and remedy provisions.

## Coverage
Identify who brings the underlying claim, who receives protection, who pays, the causal trigger, covered losses and exclusions. First-party claims and nonparty claims are different; the word indemnify alone does not conclusively select either. For IP claims, identify the product/material, rights, territory, authorized use and any exclusions for combinations, modifications, customer instructions or outdated versions. Check whether an exclusion swallows the intended use case.

Do not infer coverage from a section heading or assume a form supplies an omitted variable. If a complete agreement expressly makes coverage depend on a blank selection, describe that consequence. If the selection document is missing, request it instead of declaring a confirmed omission.

## Procedure
- Notice: trigger and deadline; does late notice forfeit protection or only affect it to the extent of prejudice? Do not invent a prejudice qualification absent from the contract.
- Defense: who appoints counsel, pays costs as incurred, controls strategy, and handles conflicts? “Defend” is not a complete procedure.
- Settlement: consent for admissions, nonmonetary obligations, unreleased claims or costs borne by the protected party; check whether consent can be withheld and on what basis.
- Cooperation: scope, expense and access to information; preserve confidentiality/privilege where relevant.
- Failure to act: what happens if defense is refused or delayed? Identify any explicit step-in rights and cost consequences. Where absent, propose negotiated procedures if within scope; do not assert a universal implied right to reimbursement.
- Product remedy: obtaining rights, modifying/replacing the product, termination/refund and service continuity. Determine whether these supplement or replace claim protection.

## Negotiation
Match the change to the represented party's approved objective. Keeping supplier defense control while requiring consent for customer admissions is different from transferring all control. Preserve existing agreed allocations unless the new change undermines them.

After an edit, recheck covered-claim definitions, liability cap/carveouts, excluded losses, exclusive remedy, notice and survival. Determine whether defense costs erode the same cap as judgments and settlements or have separate treatment; request clarification if the text does not resolve a material uncertainty.

Drafting commentary such as Adams is useful for precision but is not governing law or an instruction to remove familiar terminology from every negotiated contract. See [sources](../sources.md).
