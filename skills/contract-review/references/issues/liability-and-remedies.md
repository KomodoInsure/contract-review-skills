# Liability and remedies

Build a claim-to-remedy map before characterizing the cap. For each material failure scenario identify: claimant and defendant; duty breached; loss asserted; available remedy; exclusive-remedy restriction; monetary cap and aggregation; damages exclusions; exceptions; precedence; and survival. Do not add losses to the map that deal facts do not plausibly support.

## Cap calculation
Record the fee basis (paid/payable, affected order/all orders), time window, anchor date, per-event versus aggregate treatment, currency and any floor or separate cap. If the contract supplies numbers, calculate using those facts. Otherwise explain the formula and missing inputs. A “2×” cap is not meaningful without the base. Distinguish a shared aggregate from caps that can stack across claim types.

## Separate switches
A monetary-cap carveout and an exception to an excluded-damages clause operate independently unless linked. A claim can be uncapped but still subject to excluded heads of loss, or subject to a supercap while indirect losses remain recoverable. “Unlimited liability” should not imply every conceivable loss is recoverable.

Read misconduct, confidentiality, data and indemnity exceptions through their defined scope. A broad confidentiality definition can expand a carveout; a narrow claim definition can limit it. Do not infer that all cyber incidents necessarily breach every confidentiality or security obligation.

An exclusive remedy may restrict recovery even when a generous cap exists. Determine what event it covers and whether termination, indemnity, injunction or other remedies remain available. Distinguish recovery of fees, service credits, replacement costs and ordinary damages.

## Resolution
Use approved objectives to choose among narrowing the trigger, setting a separate cap, preserving a remedy, adjusting a fee base or escalating an exception. A lower cap and a narrower trigger are not interchangeable protections. Avoid rewriting all allocation terms when one coherent change resolves the issue.

Insurance is separate evidence. A certificate, contractual insurance requirement or stated policy limit does not establish available coverage for a particular loss. Check policy wording, exclusions, deductibles, aggregate erosion, endorsements and applicable excess layers when those documents are supplied; otherwise identify what remains unverified.

## Legal interpretation
Do not assume labels such as consequential, direct, negligence or liquidated damages have universal effects. Consult applicable law when the conclusion depends on them. The Triple Point example shows why performance obligations, termination and carveouts must be read together; it does not prescribe a standard cap or establish the result for another agreement.
