# Warranties and AI accuracy

Use for requested expansions of a provider's warranty: accuracy or error-free AI output, fitness for purpose, compliance with "all laws," incorporating RFP responses, proposals or sales materials, and deleting disclaimers. A customer-side reviewer can use it in reverse to ask for promises that are measurable and enforceable.

## Read first

- The current warranty, its remedy (repair, re-perform, refund or termination, and whether that remedy is exclusive), the disclaimer, the entire-agreement clause and precedence. See [liability](../issues/liability-and-remedies.md).
- What the AI feature actually does. Does it suggest output that a user reviews and accepts, or act automatically? Is there measured performance, and on what data, metric and method? Ask the product owner; do not adopt a marketing figure.
- Which documents the change would bring in (RFP responses, proposals, decks, website text), and whether those statements were accurate and verified when made.
- Whose legal obligations are in play: laws that apply to the provider in operating the service, versus laws that apply to the customer's business and its use of the output.
- Authority: warranties are often a non-negotiable or product-owned term, and capability statements may need business or product approval.

## What drives the decision

A warranty converts a description into a liability trigger. The risk depends on whether the promised standard is defined, within the provider's control and stated in the agreement.

- **Documentation warranty:** routine. Naming an AI feature as part of the Service clarifies rather than expands it if the documentation describes it accurately as assistive and subject to user review.
- **Accuracy or error-free output:** probabilistic systems cannot meet an absolute standard, and where the customer accepts or edits output, the provider does not control the final result. Reject absolute terms. If the customer relies on a level of performance, the alternative is a defined, measured capability (metric, test set, measurement window, remedy) placed in the documentation or service description with the right approval, not a general accuracy warranty.
- **Fitness for the customer's purpose:** turns the customer's unstated needs into the standard. Narrow to described functionality or documented use cases.
- **"Comply with all applicable laws":** can import the customer's regulatory obligations and its use of the output. Counter with compliance with laws applicable to the provider in providing the service. Address sector requirements specifically if they are real.
- **Incorporating RFP responses or sales materials:** usually the most dangerous change. It imports an open-ended set of statements, including aspirational figures, into the warranty, defeats the entire-agreement clause and creates conflicts in precedence. Decline incorporation; offer to write the specific capabilities the customer relies on into the agreement. Separately, if a pre-contract statement was inaccurate, flag it internally: an entire-agreement clause may not fully shield misrepresentation claims (research the governing law).
- **Deleting disclaimers** such as "no particular result": read it together with the accuracy ask; the deletion lets outcome-based claims back in. Restore it. Also check whether the applicable law imposes form requirements on implied-warranty disclaimers.

Facts that reverse the answer: performance that is measured and stable, an automated feature with no human review (stronger case for a defined service level), a regulated use where the customer must obtain specific commitments, or a provider that wrote the statement into its own published documentation.

## Common positions (negotiating context, not policy)

SaaS providers commonly warrant material conformity with documentation, with repair, then refund-and-terminate as the exclusive remedy. AI features are typically described as assistive and disclaimed as to accuracy, with human review expected. Compliance warranties usually cover laws applicable to the provider's performance. Customers with leverage obtain specific documented capabilities, no-material-degradation commitments or notice of material model changes, rather than accuracy guarantees. RFP incorporation appears mainly in public-sector or heavily procured deals, often with precedence below the agreement.

## Worked example

Packet: an enterprise customer ($220,000 a year) edits the warranty to say that the Service, "including [the AI feature]," will (ii) produce accurate and error-free mappings, (iii) comply with all applicable laws and (iv) meet the provider's RFP response, which is incorporated. It also deletes "does not warrant that Customer will achieve any particular result." The RFP said the feature "accurately maps 95%+ of columns automatically." Suggestions apply only after a user accepts them. The agreement has an entire-agreement clause. Policy: warranties are non-negotiable; the agreement owner may add compliance with laws applicable to the provider; a specific relied-on capability may go into the Documentation with business-owner approval.

Decision: (i) naming the feature is harmless if the Documentation describes it accurately; accept, or answer that it is already part of the Service. (ii) Reject: an absolute accuracy standard for a suggestion tool whose output the user accepts. (iii) Counter: "Provider will comply with laws applicable to Provider in providing the Service." (iv) Reject: this drives the decision, because it imports every RFP statement, including the 95% figure, and defeats the entire-agreement clause. Restore the disclaimer. Offer to document a specific capability the customer relies on, subject to business-owner approval and to the product owner confirming that it is measured and true. A 95% claim needs a defined metric and test basis before any version appears in the agreement. Internally, verify the RFP figure; if it cannot be supported, raise the misrepresentation exposure with counsel. Reverse: an auto-applied feature or a verified, stable benchmark would make a defined performance commitment worth pricing. Other concessions in this term would go outside the playbook and escalate.
