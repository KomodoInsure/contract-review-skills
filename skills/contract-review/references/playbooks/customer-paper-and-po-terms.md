# Customer paper, purchase orders and portal terms

Scope: terms that arrive through a purchase order, supplier-registration or procurement portal, invoice platform or click-through after (or instead of) a negotiated agreement, plus a short triage for deciding whether to review on customer paper or the provider's paper.

## Read first
- The signed packet: order form, main agreement, amendments, and any precedence, entire-agreement, amendment-formality ("signed by both parties") and order-document clauses. Quote the exact clause that addresses PO, portal or online terms; do not assume one exists.
- The document now in front of the user: what it is (PO, vendor-onboarding form, portal acceptance screen, in-product click-through, invoice-platform terms), who issued it, when, and what it says about precedence. Capture the exact acceptance wording and the version of the incorporated terms.
- Whether the commercial fields match the signed order: legal entities, product, quantity, fees, term, payment period, services/SOW scope, ship-to or delivery items.
- Who is being asked to accept (AE, AP clerk, end user) and whether the company treats that click as signing authority. Separately, what the user actually needs: to get paid, to get registered, or to change the deal.
- Any separate factual certifications in the portal (insurance limits, code-of-conduct, security questionnaire, diversity or tax status). These are representations to answer accurately and route to their owners, not boilerplate to click through.

## What drives the decision
A post-signature PO or portal step is usually an administrative payment mechanism. When the signed agreement has a clause saying pre-printed or online terms in POs, registrations or portals have no effect even if acknowledged or accepted, and the PO's commercial fields match the order, the ordinary answer is: proceed, but create a record that the click is administrative and the signed agreement governs. Redlining portal terms is usually the wrong move; AP rarely has authority to negotiate them, and marking them up implies they matter.

The risk is reduced, not eliminated. The later document often claims to "govern and prevail over any other agreement". Whether a later click-through can override an earlier no-effect clause, an entire-agreement clause or an amendment-formality requirement depends on governing law, the exact wording of both documents, the clicker's authority and the parties' conduct. Do not present the outcome of that contest (or of a battle of the forms where no signed agreement exists) as settled; say the provider has a strong documentary position and describe what strengthens it.

Act rather than proceed when: the PO differs from the order (price, quantity, term, entity, extra deliverables or services); the signed packet has no no-effect clause, or the order form itself incorporates the customer's PO terms; there is no signed agreement and the PO is the first or only paper (now it is a contract-formation question, escalate); the portal requires uploading a signed supplier agreement or an officer's acceptance; the customer says the terms are meant to apply; the customer is a government or regulated buyer whose mandatory flow-down clauses may apply regardless of a private precedence clause; or the incorporated terms touch services under an SOW, where "buyer owns all deliverables" collides with background IP (see [IP](../issues/intellectual-property.md)).

Payment terms are an operational risk even when legally inert: AP systems pay on the portal's configured terms. Invoice on the signed terms, cite the order and PO number, and ask AP to set the vendor record to the agreed period.

Customer-side mirror: a customer reviewer faces the same problem with the provider's URL terms that "may be updated from time to time", in-product click-throughs accepted by end users, and online policies incorporated by reference. Seek a precedence clause that the negotiated agreement controls over any click-through or later-posted terms, fixed or notice-and-exit treatment for material updates, and a statement that user clicks do not bind the customer to new terms. See [SaaS](../contract-types/saas.md) on reading the packet together.

Triage of customer paper vs our paper: customer procurement forms are often written for goods or on-site services (acceptance testing, title, buyer-owned work product, unlimited liability, audit, unilateral policy updates) and fit multi-tenant SaaS poorly. Weigh deal size and review cost against how far the form is from SaaS. Options in rough order of efficiency: provider paper with a short customer addendum; customer paper with a provider SaaS rider that expressly prevails over conflicting terms; full markup of customer paper. A rider protects only as far as its precedence clause reaches, so check that later POs, SOWs or policies cannot override it.

## Common positions (negotiating context, not policy)
Enterprise SaaS paper commonly includes a no-effect or order-of-precedence clause covering POs and portals, and many buyers accept a PO as administrative once a master agreement is signed. Some buyer portal terms already yield to "a separately signed agreement covering the same subject"; check before escalating. Buyers will often add a PO note or header field such as "governed by the [agreement] dated [date]". Customers commonly resist provider URL terms that change unilaterally and accept them only with notice and an exit or a no-material-degradation commitment.

## Worked example
Packet: enterprise CSA and order form signed last week, $150,000 per year, net 30. CSA §14.4: pre-printed or online terms in any PO, vendor registration or procurement portal have no effect even if acknowledged or accepted. AP requires portal registration and PO acceptance before paying; the PO amount and term match the order. The acceptance screen says the buyer's Standard Terms "govern and prevail over any other agreement" and include unlimited supplier liability, net 90 and buyer ownership of all deliverables. The AE asks whether to click or redline. Policy: none supplied beyond the CSA.

Decision: tell the AE they can accept to get paid, without redlining, after confirming the PO entity, product, $150,000 amount and term match the order. Steps: enter "Issued under and governed by the Cloud Service Agreement and Order Form dated [date]; per CSA §14.4 these portal terms have no effect" in any comment or attachment field; screenshot the acceptance screen and save the terms version; invoice at net 30 citing the PO number and order form; email the customer's procurement contact the same one-line confirmation. Stakes if the portal terms were treated as operative: the CSA's 12-month cap (about $150,000) would give way to unlimited liability, and net 90 would delay $150,000 of cash by 60 days. Explain that §14.4 is a strong but not guaranteed answer to the "prevails" language, because the effect of a later click depends on Delaware law and the CSA's amendment terms, and confirm with the agreement owner that AE portal clicks are an accepted practice. Reverses if: the PO amount, scope or term differs from the order; the PO adds implementation services (buyer-owned deliverables then matter); the customer states it intends the portal terms to apply; or the customer is a government buyer with mandatory terms. In those cases, hold the click and escalate to the agreement owner.
