# Customer indemnity for data, inputs and use

Scope: the customer's obligation to defend and pay for third-party claims arising from data or inputs the customer supplies and from its use of the service in breach of the acceptable-use terms, including requests to delete, narrow or cap it. Read with [indemnification](../issues/indemnification.md) for coverage and procedure.

## Read first
- The customer indemnity's covered claims (third-party only?), triggers (data content, the provider's processing of it, AUP breach, violation of law), exclusions and procedure, and the provider indemnity it mirrors. Note what the provider indemnity excludes: if it carves out customer data, deleting the customer indemnity leaves those claims with no defense at all.
- The liability clause: whether indemnities sit under the general cap, a super-cap or uncapped, and whether the indirect-damages exclusion carves out indemnity obligations for both parties.
- What the customer will actually put into the service and where it came from: its own first-party records, purchased or scraped lists, regulated categories (health, financial, children's, biometric), third-party content or code, prompts and files sent to AI features.
- How the provider processes it: only to provide the service, or also for improvement, model training, or by AI subprocessors. Customer resistance is more reasonable where the provider's own discretionary use creates the risk (see [AI training and data use](ai-training-and-data-use.md)).
- The customer's legal capacity: some public bodies cannot give indemnities under applicable law, which is a different problem from refusing on principle.

## What drives the decision
The customer indemnity allocates risks only the customer controls: whether it had the rights, notices and consents to collect and submit the data, and how its users behave. The provider cannot verify consent chains or content at scale, yet as the processor or host it is a natural defendant in privacy, marketing, IP and consumer claims. That is the answer to "we don't indemnify vendors" and "your indemnity covers your product; ours shouldn't exist": each party covers what it controls, and the provider's IP indemnity already excludes customer data for the same reason.

Narrowing is usually acceptable; deletion changes risk. Routine narrowings: third-party claims only; tie data claims to infringement or violation of law (including collection without required notice or consent) by the data itself or by the provider's processing in accordance with the agreement; exclude claims to the extent caused by the provider's breach, its processing outside the agreement or its own security failure; make procedure and cap treatment mutual. Narrowings that hollow it out: fault limited to gross negligence or willful misconduct; final judgments only (no defense costs); excluding consent and privacy-law claims, which for many customers are the main risk. Replacement language such as "each party is responsible for its own acts and omissions" does not create a defense obligation; its effect on liability allocation is uncertain and may depend on governing law, so do not treat it as an equivalent.

Weight rises with the data source and use: purchased contact lists, consumer marketing, regulated data, user-generated content, and AI inputs the customer did not create. It falls for a customer uploading its own operational data with little personal data, though it remains the backstop if that changes. For AI features, separate inputs (customer risk) from outputs generated by the provider's model (provider or model-vendor risk); a customer should not indemnify for outputs merely because it requested them (see [AI output indemnity](ai-output-indemnity.md)).

Facts that change the answer: the customer is legally barred from indemnifying (fall back to a representation and covenant on rights and consents, AUP compliance, suspension rights and direct-damages recovery, and escalate); the provider uses customer data beyond providing the service (narrow the indemnity to exclude that use, or fix the use); the customer accepts the indemnity only under a cap that is lower than the provider's (assess against [indemnity caps](indemnity-caps.md)).

## Common positions (negotiating context, not policy)
Enterprise SaaS paper commonly includes a customer indemnity for customer data and AUP breach, and many enterprise customers accept it once narrowed to third-party claims and to the provider's authorized processing. Customers commonly ask for mutual procedure, cap parity with the provider's indemnity, and exclusion of claims caused by the provider. Large or public-sector customers more often refuse or are barred; the usual substitute is a rights-and-consents warranty plus AUP and suspension rights.

## Worked example
Packet: new enterprise customer, a marketing-analytics firm importing purchased consumer contact lists; $210,000 annual fees. Redline replaces §12.2 (Customer defends Provider against third-party claims arising from Customer Protected Data or AUP breach) with "Each party is responsible for its own acts and omissions." Comment: "We don't give indemnities to vendors. Your indemnity covers your product; ours shouldn't exist." §12.1 excludes Customer Protected Data from Provider's indemnity; §11(a) caps all claims, indemnities included, at fees paid or payable in the prior 12 months; §11(c) carves indemnity obligations out of the indirect-damages exclusion. Policy: §12.2 may be narrowed to third-party claims that Customer Protected Data, or Provider's processing of it in accordance with the Agreement, infringes rights or violates law (including collection without required notice or consent), plus AUP breach; it may get the same cap treatment as §12.1; it may not be deleted.

Decision: reject the deletion; it is outside policy and, for this customer, removes cover for the most likely claim, consumers or regulators alleging the purchased lists lacked required notice or consent, with Provider named as processor. Counter with the narrowed policy text and offer explicit cap parity: both indemnities already sit under §11(a), about $210,000 at the current annual fee, and both are carved out of §11(c). Response: "Our indemnity covers what we control, the Service; we already exclude your data from it. This one covers only what you control: the rights and consents behind data you choose to upload and compliance with the AUP, and only for third-party claims about our processing as the Agreement permits. It has the same cap as ours." Reverses if: the customer is legally barred from indemnifying, or insists on deletion after the narrowed counter; either escalates to VP Sales, as deletion is beyond the agreement owner's authority.
