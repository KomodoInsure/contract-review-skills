# Insurance alignment

Use when a draft adds or edits an insurance clause (required types and limits, additional insured, primary/non-contributory, waiver of subrogation, cancellation notice, certificates), or when a counterparty asks the represented party to confirm that its insurance covers its contractual liability.

## Read first
- The coverage actually carried, by policy: type, limit, per-claim or per-occurrence versus aggregate, whether a limit is combined across lines (for example cyber and technology E&O on one tower) and shared across all customers, retention or deductible, and any excess layers. Get it from the broker or certificate summary; do not infer it from the contract.
- Which endorsements the broker says are available: additional insured by line, primary/non-contributory, waiver of subrogation, notice of cancellation to third parties. Availability often differs by line; liability and cyber/E&O policies are frequently treated differently.
- The exposure the agreement creates: compute each cap tier and uncapped indemnity from deal facts and set it against the relevant limit and retention. See [liability and remedies](../issues/liability-and-remedies.md) and [indemnity caps](indemnity-caps.md).
- The approved insurance position and who can authorize buying more coverage.

## What drives the decision
An insurance clause is a covenant the represented party must perform every day of the term. The first question is not whether a limit is reasonable but whether the party carries it, in the form described. Committing to a type, limit or endorsement not carried is a breach at signature and can be a default trigger; it cannot be fixed by later buying coverage without a business decision and premium.

Routine when they match what is carried: listing types and limits; providing certificates on request; additional insured on the line where the broker confirms it; notice of cancellation phrased as the insured's own promise to tell the customer (insurers often will not undertake notice to certificate holders). Risk-changing: limits higher than carried; "per claim" where the policy is an annual aggregate (an aggregate shared across customers can be eroded by other claims before this customer's claim); additional insured on cyber or E&O where unavailable; primary/non-contributory and waiver of subrogation without broker confirmation, since each depends on an endorsement and may alter how the policy responds; clauses stating that insurance limits are a floor on liability or that liability is limited to insurance proceeds.

Requests to "confirm insurance covers your liability under this Agreement" should be declined as asked. Whether a policy responds to a particular loss depends on its wording, exclusions, retention, remaining aggregate and the facts of the claim; no contract reviewer can confirm it, and a confirmation can be read as a representation. Answer with what is carried, offer certificates, and say that liability is governed by the limitation clause, not by insurance. Coverage conclusions stay conditional on policy wording.

Reverses when: the broker confirms the endorsement or limit is in place or can be added and the business owner approves the cost; the customer accepts an aggregate or combined-limit description; the exposure is within limits and the clause merely describes coverage. On the customer side, the same facts matter in reverse: an aggregate shared across all of a vendor's customers and a large retention reduce what a stated limit is worth, so ask for the structure rather than the headline number.

## Common positions
Negotiating context only, not the user's policy. Customers often ask for cyber and E&O limits in the low-to-high millions, additional insured status, primary/non-contributory wording and subrogation waivers; vendors commonly grant additional insured and waivers on general liability more readily than on professional or cyber lines, and describe combined or aggregate limits as they are. Do not treat these as approved positions.

## Company position (fill in)
- Standard: [types and limits carried; certificates on request]
- Acceptable fallback: [endorsements available by line; cancellation-notice wording]
- Walk-away: [commitments to limits or terms not carried; representations that insurance covers a liability]
- Who approves: [business owner and broker for new or higher coverage]

Apply these as policy only when the user supplies them; otherwise label any proposal a negotiating position.

## Worked example
Packet: new enterprise customer; fees $600,000/year. General cap $600,000; Increased Claims cap 5.0x = $3,000,000. Carried: $5,000,000 annual aggregate combined cyber and tech E&O, shared across all customers, $100,000 retention; CGL $1,000,000 per occurrence/$2,000,000 aggregate; additional insured available on CGL only. Customer's §16 requires $10,000,000 cyber per claim, $5,000,000 tech E&O per claim, CGL $1M/$2M, additional insured on all policies, primary/non-contributory, waiver of subrogation, and 30 days' insurer notice of cancellation; counsel asks Provider to confirm insurance covers its liability. Policy: describe coverage actually carried; certificates on request; additional insured on CGL only; no commitments not carried; no representations of coverage; buying more is a business-owner decision.

Decision: counter §16; the driver is the limits commitment, since (a) is double the carried limit and (a) and (b) together describe $15,000,000 of per-claim cover against one shared $5,000,000 aggregate. Accept (c). Restate (a)-(b) as the combined $5,000,000 annual aggregate; limit additional insured to CGL; strike primary/non-contributory and waiver of subrogation unless the broker confirms the endorsements, and if confirmed limit them to CGL; recast notice as Provider's promise to notify Customer if required coverage is cancelled without replacement. Route any request for higher limits to the business owner and broker. The $3,000,000 cap is below the stated limit, but that does not establish coverage. Reply: "Provider maintains the coverage described in revised §16 and will provide certificates on request. Whether any policy responds to a particular claim depends on its terms, so we cannot confirm coverage of specific liabilities; Provider's liability is governed by Section 11." Reverses if the broker confirms higher limits or endorsements and the business owner approves the cost.
