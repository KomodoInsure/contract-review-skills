# Worked review contrasts

All scenarios and policies here are hypothetical. They teach a procedure, not universal acceptable positions.

## A cap and a separate exclusion
Packet: total liability is capped at $100,000; confidentiality breaches are excluded from that cap. A separate clause excludes lost profits for every claim without exception. Policy: confidentiality may be uncapped, but no lost-profit exposure is authorized.

Decision: the uncapped confidentiality treatment does not itself remove the separate lost-profits exclusion. Accept the stated allocation if the rest of the packet does not change it. Do not “repair” the damages clause by adding a confidentiality exception: that would create the very exposure the policy prohibits.

Change the second clause to exclude confidentiality from its scope: now flag the policy deviation and target that exception. Remove the second clause from an incomplete packet: identify the missing evidence, not a confirmed violation.

## A deletion promise and backups
Packet: delete all copies within ten days. Verified capability: active copies can be deleted in ten days, routine backups rotate within 90 days. Policy permits a narrow backup exception with continued confidentiality and no ordinary use.

Decision: preserve the active-system deadline; propose a bounded backup exception and clarify restoration handling. Counterparty comment: explain the technical rotation and continued protection. Do not disclose an internal fallback maximum. If the technical evidence is missing, ask the storage owner rather than making up a 90-day schedule.

## Ownership with a missing license
Packet: customer owns custom deliverables, supplier retains embedded tools, no license permits use of those tools. Customer needs to operate and maintain the deliverables after exit.

Decision: seek the necessary continuing license for embedded background material. Do not transfer all supplier IP as a shortcut. Recheck permitted maintainers, modification, sublicensing and termination survival. If an adequate license is already present in an exhibit, accept it rather than duplicating it in the ownership clause.

## Cover-page evidence
Packet: standard terms say indemnity exists only for Covered Claims selected on the cover page. Cover page missing.

Decision: coverage is unverified; request the cover page. Do not infer a default IP indemnity. If the complete signed cover page leaves coverage blank, identify the resulting absence against the supplied policy. If it selects the required coverage, check its exclusions and cap rather than declaring it missing.

## Renewal timing
Packet: non-renewal is due 60 days before renewal; price increases may be announced 30 days before renewal. Customer policy requires an exit opportunity after learning the new price.

Decision: propose earlier price notice or an express post-notice cancellation right. Both may work; compare operational consequences. Check deemed delivery and business/calendar-day definitions. Do not set arbitrary dates without the renewal date.

## Governing law moved between US states
Packet: an enterprise prospect returns the provider's cloud agreement with governing law changed from Delaware to New York; forum stays in US courts. Policy: any US state's law and US courts are acceptable for enterprise customers; customers on standard terms get no legal redlines; non-US law or forum for the main agreement needs escalation. The reviewer may accept changes within policy.

Decision: accept on substance and within authority. New York is a common, well-developed commercial law and the provider's protections remain. Still compare the rest of the redline before calling the draft final. The answer reverses for a customer on standard terms or a non-US law or forum. A DPA carrying different law for data-protection reasons is not a reason to reopen this change.

## Raised cap with a vague new trigger
Packet: at renewal, an enterprise customer raises the increased-liability cap from 5x to the greater of $1 million or 10x trailing fees, extends it to any breach of the security section, and adds "material misrepresentation of the Service". Policy permits up to 10x with a $1 million floor within insurance limits and requires each increased category to be tied to a defined contractual obligation. The agreement disclaims warranties not stated in it and has an entire-agreement clause.

Decision: do not accept as sent, and say why precisely. The cap, floor and security-section category are within policy. Exposure rises, but that is a priced, insured risk. The misrepresentation category is the problem: it is undefined, can reach sales statements outside the agreement, and conflicts with the warranty and entire-agreement provisions. Strike it and offer to write any specific capability the customer relies on into the service description. Do not lead with the cap or present all three changes as equal concerns.

## Customer-run audit clause in a DPA
Packet: a new customer edits the DPA so the provider must allow audits, inspections and technical testing by the customer or a customer-chosen auditor at least yearly. Policy: audit duties are met by an annual independent audit (such as SOC 2) by an auditor the provider selects, reports shared under NDA; no customer-run audits or testing of shared systems. No law applicable to this customer's data requires direct customer audits.

Decision: reject the clause, not merely trim it. Explain that independent certified audits already provide the assurance, and that one customer choosing auditors or testing shared infrastructure would put every customer's data at risk. Offer the policy's alternatives: the latest report under NDA, priority on a certification the customer needs, or dedicated hosting. If applicable law did require direct audit or regulator access, identify that requirement and escalate instead of drafting a concession the policy does not authorize.
