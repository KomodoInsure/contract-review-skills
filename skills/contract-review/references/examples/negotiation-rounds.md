# Negotiation-round review

Use for a returned draft with negotiation history. The client’s mandate determines what needs changing; a generally stronger protection is not automatically a better response in a late round.

## Decide what to reopen

Separate open counterparty questions, mandatory client protections, settled positions, and repairs needed to make the selected changes work. Follow any explicit ordering of priorities, such as closing with minimum redlining while protecting named risks. Silence on a negotiating point is neither proof of approval nor a universal prohibition on proposing it. Under a minimum-redline mandate, avoid adding unrelated affirmative asks merely because they would improve the client’s starting position; explain a material new ask and its reason rather than presenting it as a settled requirement.

History is clause-specific. If the open issue concerns one dimension of a licence or remedy, preserve other accepted dimensions unless the current mandate or changed exposure supplies a reason to reopen them. An explanation of the requested change should also disclose any material additional change. Do not treat a counterparty proposal as approval by the represented party.

## Repair the connected result

A form drafted for another transaction may be adapted through targeted deletion, redefinition or an express scope limitation. Choose the coherent approach that fits this client’s mandate. Check surviving uses before deleting a definition. A scope limitation is insufficient if it leaves a materially applicable obligation or prerequisite in conflict; conversely, retained text is not necessarily an omission when clearly disapplied.

A changed definition can leave a location, deadline or approval mechanism indeterminate. Do not substitute an invented commercial or jurisdictional choice. If the needed value will be supplied in another document, identify the exact dependency, what must be agreed, and when it is required; distinguish a proposed draft with an open decision from a completed operative arrangement. An agreement to choose later does not itself supply the missing value.

Assess the final provision together with relevant definitions, priority and remedies, wherever the operative language appears. Where implicated, check that a post-termination duty survives, that a later document’s precedence clause cannot silently defeat the fix, and that the remedy’s fee base and cap still provide the intended recovery when the trigger occurs. For example, a refund limited to fees for a remaining term can provide nothing after that term ends. Do not assume an exception to a cap also overrides a loss exclusion. Placement alone is not a substantive failure unless the mandate fixes it or the move changes meaning, priority or usability. Keep explanations concise and specific to the actual change.

## Contrasting examples

These facts are hypothetical, not approved playbook positions.

- Counsel approved a deal-specific cap; the counterparty retains it and fixes a typo. Preserve the cap rather than reopening it to match a default.
- Approval covered third-party IP claims; the returned draft extends it to a different claim class and removes a damages exclusion. Compare the changed allocation, not just the unchanged cap amount.
- The client requested a territorial licence correction; another licence restriction was already accepted. Repair the territorial issue without silently broadening the accepted restriction.
- A document-specific definition is replaced with a service definition. Follow every operative dependency affected by that replacement; identify an unresolved venue or timing input rather than claiming the conversion is complete.
- Internal fallback authority permits a concession. Explain the commercial reason externally without revealing the internal maximum unless authorised.

## Expansion mandate

A customer approved a narrow internal-use licence before adding a maintenance contractor and a subsidiary. The client now authorizes changes needed for their use and a transition if support ends. Preserve ownership of the supplier’s standalone materials, but draft affirmative use/access rights for the authorized users, their scope and conditions, and the rights and practical handover needed after support or the agreement ends. If a promised post-term defect remedy returns only unearned fees for an expired term, repair the remedy as well as the warranty. Identify any unverified third-party licence as a condition to proceeding; a promise to agree terms later does not supply rights today. Do not call the package complete while those dependencies remain open, or substitute blanket restraint for the expanded mandate.

## Return a usable round

Follow the requested deliverable. Ordinarily provide the anchored changes, their external rationales and the client decisions, with accepted issues briefly closed. Do not repeat each rationale in a second findings inventory. For requested send-ready text, separate the operative block from internal alternatives and authority limits. A required open value makes the affected draft conditional, even when its placeholder is easy to spot. Compare each rationale against the actual text for additional rights, deadlines, restrictions or concessions. Answer a counterparty’s factual question conditionally when the needed licence, approval or evidence has not been supplied.
